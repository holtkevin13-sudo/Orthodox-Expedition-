## ✦ Vocabulary — Match Each Word to Its Meaning

Draw a line from each word on the left to its meaning on the right.

  Eucharist             •     •  Greek for *bread set aside* — the small
                                 piece of blessed bread (not consecrated)
                                 you take after the chalice. A sign of
                                 fellowship in the Church.

  Real Presence         •     •  The cup that holds the consecrated
                                 wine — the Blood of Christ. Father Nick
                                 carries it through the Holy Doors at
                                 the moment of Communion.

  Chalice               •     •  Greek for *thanksgiving* — the central
                                 Sacrament of the Orthodox Church, the
                                 reason the Liturgy exists.

  Antidoron             •     •  The Orthodox teaching that Christ is
                                 truly present in the bread and wine —
                                 not as a symbol or memorial, but truly
                                 the Body and Blood of the Lord.

## ✦ Sequence — Approaching the Chalice

Every Sunday for the rest of your life, you will approach the chalice in this exact order. Number these from **1** to **6** in the boxes below.

  ☐  Take a small piece of bread (antidoron) from the deacon's basket.

  ☐  Cross your arms over your chest, right over left.

  ☐  Walk forward in the line when the choir sings *With fear of God, with faith and love, draw near.*

  ☐  Tell Father Nick your name quietly: *Nolan*.

  ☐  Receive the Body and Blood of Christ on the spoon. Don't bite the spoon.

  ☐  Step to the side. Be still for a moment. Then return to your place.

## ✦ Reflection — Write 3 to 5 Sentences

The lesson said receiving the Eucharist is the most sacred thing you will ever do — and that most Sundays it will not feel dramatic. Both true. Write 3 to 5 sentences about your first Communion last Sunday (or the Sunday before). What do you remember most? Did it feel like the most sacred thing you have ever done — or did it feel ordinary, or somewhere in between? *All of those are honest. The truth of what you received does not depend on how it felt.*
